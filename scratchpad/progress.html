LEM Web Server · the floor

The map becomes a working railway

The lab floor was an SVG drawing: isometric boxes for instruments, dots running down pipes when a parser parsed. It is a rendered site now — procedural facilities in forest, real weather and global illumination, and a petroleum working out to the LabCore terminal on every parse. The second gauntlet judges behaviour rather than pictures: trains that never touch, a railway that routes itself through any layout, and ground that runs to the horizon without an edge.

The line

Each subsystem is one file with one owner, built and judged on its own. A subsystem that fails to load is skipped and the map carries on without it — the floor is a status display before it is a rendering.

Engine, camera, materials

clear world/engine.js · camera.js · textures.js

Deferred-ish pipeline: HDR target, half-res horizon-based AO with a depth-aware blur, bloom, ACES composite with grade and grain, FXAA. A five-step quality ladder that walks itself down on a slow GPU. Every texture is generated on the client — nothing is downloaded.

The page seam

clear templates/floor.html · static/world/index.js

430 lines of SVG renderer removed. drawFloor() and spawnBlip() kept their names, so all eighteen call sites still mean what they meant. Every rail, dialog, menu and poll on the page is untouched. The 2D toggle is gone, not hidden.

Sky & atmosphere

first draft world/sky.js

Scattering model, real dawn and dusk, cloud cover that thickens to overcast, and the environment map everything else is lit by.

Global illumination

first draft world/gi.js

Fitted sun shadows plus an irradiance probe grid with ground bounce, injected into materials so indirect light has a direction.

Terrain & water

first draft world/terrain.js

A forested valley, level pads cut for each instrument, a graded rail formation, splatted ground materials, and puddles that fill when it rains.

Station buildings

first draft world/buildings.js

One procedurally generated petroleum facility per instrument, stable from its uid, plus the LabCore terminal every train runs to.

Railway

built world/rail.js

2117 lines, rebuilt from scratch against the track close-up reference: rail profile with a polished head, sleepers with per-sleeper variation, ballast shoulder, turnouts, signals, relay cabinets and platform canopies.

Trains

first draft world/trains.js

One parse, one train. Tank cars with placards and weathering behind a first-generation or a modern diesel, chosen per instrument.

Forest

first draft world/vegetation.js

Instanced conifers and broadleaf, translucent backlit foliage, gusts that travel across the map, undergrowth where the camera gets close.

Weather

first draft world/weather.js

A state machine that actually evolves: rain, storm, snow, valley fog, lightning that lights the scene, and surfaces that dry slowly afterwards.

Status signage

first draft world/labels.js

The part that keeps this a status board. Name plates, a lit status band readable across a room, QC/PM/CAL kept separate, hazard stripes and smoke on a dead-lined instrument.

The soak

The visual gauntlet judged pictures. This one judges behaviour, because the faults Ryan reported are not things a screenshot shows. 500 parse events across ten machine layouts — the lab's real one, a rank, a file, sparse scatter, and two instruments deliberately sharing one bay.

Trains running into each other

0 / 500 world/trains.js · rail.js

Block reservation on the OpenTTD model: a working may only enter track it has reserved, and a reservation is refused rather than negotiated, so a collision cannot happen rather than happening rarely. Baseline reproduced the fault — slot 0 ran straight through slot 1 standing at the origin.

Trains going back through the railway

0 / 500 world/trains.js

A return working takes the loop or the other road; it never retraces the track it came up.

The massive lip at the map edge

0 faults world/terrain.js

The soak walks outward on eight bearings looking for a step no landscape would have. It found them on every layout — 26 to 72 metres, at 400 to 1040 metres out. The height field is continuous to the horizon now.

Ground beneath every building

0 faults world/terrain.js · buildings.js

Every station's dock checked against the terrain height under it, in all ten layouts.

A railway that makes sense anywhere

0 unreachable world/rail.js

Every station routable in every layout, including the sparse scatter and the shared bay. A balloon loop turns the terminal, which is what real petroleum unit trains use.

Re-organising the machines

shipped templates/floor.html · world/index.js

An Arrange mode that handles the shared lock for you, draws the bays on the ground so you can see where a building will land, and lays the whole floor out at once — Grid, Compact or Row. Positions save through the same endpoint a drag uses.

Junction fouling

unchecked scratchpad/harness/soak.mjs

135,887 comparisons the harness could not make: two lines fouling each other at a shared junction. Route objects expose no way to sample a point in world space and the consist group sits at the origin. Counted and reported rather than passed over quietly — a check that silently never runs reads as a pass, which is how three earlier bugs survived.

One honest caveat about the fps number. The machine running this build has an M5 Max, not the Intel Iris Xe the target describes, so raw frames per second here would flatter the result by roughly five to eight times. The budgets above — draw calls, triangles, frame cost as a share of 16.6 ms — are what actually gets held to, and they are the numbers a slower GPU cares about. Real hardware verification needs a bench PC.
