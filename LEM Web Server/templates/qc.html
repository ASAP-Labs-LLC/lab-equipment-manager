{# THE QC WALL. Ryan: "design it to run like a literal monitor, for viewing far away, and just being locked on that screen." Three rules follow from "monitor", and every choice below is one of them. IT NEVER SCROLLS. A wall display with content below the fold is a wall display that is lying by omission. What fits, fits; what does not, rotates on a timer with a page indicator, worst first. Nobody is there to scroll. IT IS READ FROM FOUR METRES. Nothing small carries meaning. Instrument names are set large and heavy; the numbers that matter are large; the axis labels that would be unreadable at that distance are simply not drawn, because the shared scale below makes them unnecessary. THE SIGNATURE: ONE SCALE ACROSS THE WHOLE WALL. Every chart is drawn normalised to its own pass band — -1 is the lower spec limit, +1 the upper, 0 the target. So the band occupies the SAME horizontal zone on every card and a single target rule runs across the entire wall. Sixteen charts in °C, %m/m, cSt and kPa are not comparable any other way, and at this distance the eye follows one continuous line far better than it reads twenty axes. This is the lab's own metrology (deviation in band half-widths) used as the layout. Fonts are the system stack, deliberately. This screen hangs on a LAN in a laboratory and a webfont that fails to load mid-shift would take the legibility of the whole wall with it. #} LEM — QC Wall
